# Visibility in the Platform Economy: A Study of Local Cultural Content

## Abstract

This paper studies the platform economy and local culture in order to understand how visibility is produced, distributed and contested. It begins from the observation that identical cultural content performs very differently across platforms, and it asks why this should be so. The paper argues that visibility should be understood as an outcome of three interacting factors: narrative form, actor type and platform logic. Each factor is discussed at length, and each is illustrated with material drawn from public accounts on three platforms. The paper also considers how algorithmic folklorism has been used in earlier work, and it suggests that the term is too broad to guide empirical analysis. It then develops an alternative vocabulary. The analysis proceeds in four steps. First, it reviews the literature on platformisation and cultural circulation. Second, it describes the data and the coding strategy. Third, it presents four findings, each of which is connected to a research question. Fourth, it discusses the implications of these findings for cultural policy and for the study of platforms more generally. The paper concludes that visibility is not a property of content but a relational outcome, and that researchers should therefore study the arrangement of actors rather than the quality of texts. This conclusion has consequences for how cultural organisations allocate resources, and for how platforms are governed. It also has consequences for how we teach cultural policy.

Keywords: platform economy; local culture
